# B2 Czech: masculine nouns

Draft word list for the B2 picture-card deck, same format as `b1-masculine.md`.
Grouped by topic, not by frequency. Masculine splits into **animate (Ma, dark
blue)** – people and animals – and **inanimate (Mi, light blue)** – everything
else.

Every word comes from `tools/b2/candidates-m-8000.json`: the 473 masculine
nouns in ranks 4,001–8,000 of the SYN2015 frequency list that the deck (A1, A2
and B1, 1,237 cards) does not have yet, each verified as a noun on the Czech
Wiktionary. Each word is sorted into exactly one of three groups: the
**picturable core** (a person, animal, thing, place, food or scene that fits in
one small cartoon and is fine for a child), the **abstract** tier (useful B2
words that need a sentence card rather than a picture) and **left out**
(jargon, technical terms, politics, loans, colloquialisms, duplicates). B2
words are odder than the earlier levels – more knights, quarries and
greenhouses, fewer everyday rooms – and the picturable share is smaller
(about three in ten). The Rank column is the SYN2015 rank.

Patterns. Animate: **pán** (hard), **muž** (soft), **předseda** (-a),
**soudce** (-ce). Inanimate: **hrad** (hard), **stroj** (soft). A note in
parentheses gives an irregular genitive or plural; "adjective declension"
marks nouns that decline like adjectives (účetní); "plural only" marks
odpadky and rozpaky. Oddities this round: **kníže** declines like hrabě
(knížete) and is neuter in the plural (knížata); several words are animate in
one sense and inanimate in the other – **los** (moose / lottery ticket),
**drak** (dragon / kite), **jeřáb** (crane bird / crane machine), **robot**
(robota or robotu) – and the picture shows both. **ukazatel** and **nosič**
are tagged animate in the candidate file but the common senses (signpost,
roof rack) are inanimate, so they sit in Mi travel. **zápisník** is given
animate forms on the Czech Wiktionary (zápisníka), a quirk; in real use the
notebook is inanimate (do zápisníku) and the card treats it so. **vůl** is
the ox on the card; its use as an insult is not the sense drawn.

Food, meals and time all have a few words this round, so no section is
empty; the animals get an inanimate section too (ocas, dobytek).

## Notes for the drawings

- People in Ma 1 need distinct props: engineer with hard hat and cog, old
  man with stick and long beard, grandson holding grandad's hand, peasant
  farmer with pitchfork, accountant with calculator and ledger,
  archaeologist with brush and trowel. Keep them apart from the B1 architekt
  (blueprint) and A1 farmář (tractor).
- The animals (Ma 2) are the biggest single group and should share one
  style: farm pair vůl (yoke) and býk (nose ring) next to the A1 kráva;
  forest pair jelen and orel; small creatures mravenec, brouk, pavouk at the
  same scale as the A1 motýl; tygr and osel for the zoo; kocour as the
  scruffy male of the kočka card.
- Two-sense animals: los with a lottery ticket in the corner, jeřáb bird in
  front of a construction crane, drak with a kite in the sky – one picture,
  both senses, and the English column names both.
- Sport people continue the B1 football scene: záložník in the centre
  circle, běžec with a bib, cyklista on a racing bike, rybář on the bank,
  golf and terč as the equipment cards.
- Fairy-tale and history cluster in Ma other: čert (the friendly Mikuláš
  devil, black fur and chain – not a red horned demon), princ on a white
  horse, rytíř in armour, pirát with parrot, trpaslík as a garden gnome,
  mnich, kardinál in red, poutník with staff and shell, strážce in the blue
  Prague Castle sentry uniform, detektiv with magnifying glass, robot as a
  boxy tin machine.
- Church furniture: zvon, oltář, věnec (Advent), pomník, trůn – flat and
  simple so they are not confused with the A1 kostel.
- Home cluster: práh, komín, trám, žebřík, kohoutek, zvonek, závěs (heavy,
  dark, beside the thin A2 záclona), květináč, záhon, skleník; the cleaning
  trio úklid, hadr, odpadky share a bucket.
- Shapes for school: válec, kužel, trojúhelník in the same style as the A2
  kruh and čtverec; graf, zlomek and vzorec on a blackboard; zápisník is
  small and black with an elastic band, unlike the A1 sešit.
- Time pair: soumrak and úsvit are mirror images (sun setting left with a
  bat, sun rising right with a rooster); kalendář is a wall grid with one
  day circled.
- Landscape words in Mi other: obzor, útes, záliv, vodopád, ledovec, prales,
  háj, dub, lom, mlýn, příkop; keep the sea ones (obzor, útes, záliv) with
  the same blue as the A1 moře.
- Face and sound cards: křik (open mouth, sound lines) and pláč (tears and
  handkerchief) join B1 úsměv, smích, smutek, vztek.
- Words needing an agreed metaphor: vitamín (orange with a C), vir (spiky
  ball), box (gloves in a ring), trik (rabbit from a hat), návod (1-2-3
  leaflet), tucet (egg carton of twelve), jídelníček (canteen chalkboard),
  háček (fish hook beside a č), účes (an up-do with pins), průvod (masked
  carnival procession).
